problem: |
  After a major disaster such as a hurricane, earthquake, or flood, one of the first priorities is determining whether damaged buildings are safe to enter. Today, this process largely depends on inspectors visiting each structure in person, which can take days or even weeks in heavily affected areas. During that time, emergency responders, local governments, businesses, and displaced residents are left waiting for critical information needed to make decisions about safety, recovery, and resource allocation.
  
  The challenge is that manual inspections are not only time-consuming but can also place inspectors in dangerous environments. As disasters become more frequent and severe, the demand for rapid damage assessment continues to grow. Current methods often struggle to provide the speed and scale needed to support effective response efforts immediately after an event.
  
This research addresses this gap by using aerial imagery and artificial intelligence to automatically identify and classify building damage. The classification is divided into four different categories: damaged, minor damage, major damage or undamaged. By providing an early assessment of whether structures are likely safe or unsafe, the system can help decision-makers prioritize inspections, direct resources more efficiently, and accelerate recovery efforts while reducing risk to personnel in the field.


approach: |
  - Step 1 — Import the Dataset: Import the dataset that is going to be used to train the model. The dataset we are going to use is the [xBD dataset](https://xview2.org/dataset).
  - Step 2 — Train the model: We are going to use the xBD dataset to trail the damage assessment model
  - Step 3 — Evaluate & Optimize: Measure model performance using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, and refine the model through testing and hyperparameter tuning.
  - Step 4 — Build the Interface: Develop a web-based application that allows users to upload imagery and view AI-generated building safety classifications and confidence scores.
  - Step 5 — Communicate the Results: Present findings through reports, visualizations, and demonstrations that highlight the model's performance, impact, and limitations.

outcome: |
  By the end of this research, we expect to deliver a trained damage-classification model, a working web application, and a research presentation that demonstrate the feasibility of AI-assisted post-disaster building assessment. The model will be a fine-tuned convolutional neural network (CNN) capable of classifying buildings from aerial imagery as safe or unsafe according to ATC-20 standards, while the web application will allow users to upload images and receive damage assessments with confidence scores. These deliverables will be documented and presented through a research poster and presentation at the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI.
